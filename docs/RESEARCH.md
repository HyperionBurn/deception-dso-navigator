# Borrow ten proven fixes for tonight

Nobody has published a tested system that talks a remote operator through a building by voice, so there is no copy-and-paste answer. The nearest work still backs our core design and points to ten cheap changes for tonight (table below). The biggest gains are not smarter route finding. They are firm rules for when the agent is unsure, calmer turn-taking settings, a tighter audio bridge, and tests that give a pass rate. Human wayfinding studies favour landmarks over distances and compass words ([Tom and Denis 2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-39923-0_24), snippet), and typed-instruction datasets split routes into short direction-plus-landmark pieces ([Talk2Nav](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.02029), read). Our half-duplex bridge is the standard fallback when echo removal is missing. The weak spots are in the evidence, not the design: no source gives numbers for noise or music, the old ElevenLabs simulation endpoint is gone, ElevenLabs docs disagree on language handling, and our Arabic wording is not native-reviewed.

How sure is each claim: **read** = the researcher opened the page. **snippet** = seen only in a search summary, check before quoting. **inference** = the researcher's own idea, no source tests it. Notes were written 24 Nov 2026; ElevenLabs docs change fast, so confirm setting names in the dashboard.

## Where our build already matches the best practice

Most of our design has support, but nearly all of it comes from typed outdoor datasets, not spoken indoor routes. **One instruction at a time:** Talk2Nav moves to the next piece only when the aimed landmark is reached, and merging all pieces into one instruction cut its score from 11.92 to 9.71 ([Talk2Nav](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.02029), read). That was a trained model, not a person. ElevenLabs advises replies under three sentences ([prompting guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/best-practices/prompting-guide), read). NavCog3 says a step should come at the time and place the user must act, which matches giving the next step only after "done" ([NavCog3](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10304287), snippet).

**Landmarks, no compass words or distances:** one study found landmark directions gave a better mental picture than street names ([Tom and Denis](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-39923-0_24), snippet). A second found more errors with distance and compass descriptions, but the notes saw that second-hand only. Touchdown writers described landmarks and exact stopping spots ([Touchdown](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.12354), read). No source tested word order, so "landmark last" is our choice, not a finding.

**Hold and ask:** KnowNo and HANNA both treat asking for help as a planned action ([KnowNo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928), [HANNA](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01871), read, abstracts only). ElevenLabs says to admit not knowing instead of guessing. **Operator's report beats the plan:** only an analogy. Spot's docs call a seen printed marker "the easiest method" to fix the robot's position guess ([Spot](https://dev.bostondynamics.com/docs/concepts/autonomy/initialization), read). **Half duplex with a tail:** Pipecat ships a "mute while the bot speaks" strategy ([Pipecat](https://docs.pipecat.ai/pipecat/fundamentals/interruptions), read), and a vendor note says barge-in is impossible without echo removal ([XMOS](https://www.xmos.com/documentation/XM-015450-TM/html/doc/rst/building_blocks.html), snippet). Our 0.5 s tail sits at the top of the notes' 300 to 500 ms guess, which has no source. **TTS:** the models page lists `eleven_v4_turbo` for Arabic at about 100 ms ([models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models), read).

Two things the notes cannot vouch for. Nothing tests a small model like deepseek flash on these rules. Nothing says whether a stateless route tool loses anything; the chat history already holds the confirmed stops (inference). So every prompt rule needs its own test (row 5).

## Four traps: a dead endpoint, conflicting docs, no noise data, unchecked Arabic

**The dead endpoint.** ElevenLabs marked the old simulate-conversation endpoint "will be removed on 31 Oct 2026", so assume it is gone ([API reference](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/api-reference/agents/simulate-conversation.md), read). Old sample code using `simulation_specification` or `tool_mock_config` will fail. The replacement is the agent-test API, `POST /v1/convai/agents/{agent_id}/run-tests`, with a Simulation test type ([run-tests](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/api-reference/tests/run-tests.md), read). Two tool fields are also deprecated; the notes did not check whether they still work ([Create tool](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/api-reference/tools/create.md), read).

**Conflicting docs.** The language page lists "fixed language per call, no mid-call switching" as a pitfall, while the language-detection page says the agent switches voice mid-call ([Language](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/agents-platform/customization/language), [language_detection](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/tools/system-tools/language-detection), read both). Number reading has the same split: the models page says Flash v2.5 does not normalise numbers by default, while the TTS best-practices page says normalisation is on for all models ([models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models), [best practices](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices), read both). Test both on our own agent. Trust neither page.

**No noise numbers.** See the next section.

**Unreviewed Arabic.** The notes contain draft Arabic lines (start, change, wait, arrive, don't know). The researcher wrote them, and no native speaker has reviewed or listened to them. ElevenLabs documents nothing on Gulf versus Modern Standard Arabic. Forum posts on Vapi and Retell say dialect settings drift ([Vapi](https://support.vapi.ai/t/27085893/arabic-dialects), snippet, third party). So do not promise "Gulf Arabic" in the pitch, and treat any Arabic the agent says as unverified. A second model back-translating the lines is only a rough check, the notes say.

## Noise and music: only our own recordings can settle it

Every number the notes suggest here is a guess: the 300 to 500 ms tail, stricter speech-detector values (0.8 confidence, 0.3 s start), a 15 dB echo drop, and passing at 4 of 5 runs. No source covers music in speech recognition, microphone gain, or which speech model copes best. The notes also do not know whether ElevenLabs' "Filter background speech" setting handles music or only voices. What is checked is narrow. Noisy-environment detection is Enterprise only and fires only on a second voice, not music or traffic. The "background sound" setting is mixed into the agent's own voice and never reaches speech recognition ([Audio environment](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/voice/audio-environment.md), [Conversation flow](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/conversation-flow.md), read).

Real echo removal exists: LiveKit's Python package takes the speaker signal as a reference, needs a delay setting and exact 10 ms frames ([LiveKit APM](https://docs.livekit.io/reference/python/livekit/rtc/apm.html), read). The page does not say it works outside a LiveKit room, and a community post reports trouble with a 1 to 2 s delay. Keep half duplex. One more trap: browsers apply echo removal and noise suppression by default, so a browser test looks cleaner than the robot's raw mic ([dev.to guide](https://dev.to/vaiu-ai/how-to-build-reliable-barge-in-logic-for-voice-agents-k1a), secondary, snippet). The free fix is physical: ask organisers to put the music away from the robot and turn it down, and keep the robot's speaker quiet and away from its mic ([Seeed forum](https://forum.seeedstudio.com/t/respeaker-lite-aec-support/283114), snippet).

## Later / skip

- **Simulation tests with mocked tools, and a slow or broken stub for the route tool (M).** Do after row 5 if hours remain. Mocks do not simulate slowness ([Agent Testing](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/agent-testing.md), read).
- **Text-only Python test script (M).** Overlaps row 5. It skips speech, voice, echo and music, so it proves logic only ([Chat Mode](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/guides/chat-mode.md), read). Worth it only if the test judge proves soft on format.
- **`skip_turn` tool (S).** Keeps the agent quiet while the operator walks ([System tools](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/tools/system-tools), read). The model may overuse it. Try only if rehearsal shows chatter.
- **Read-back on turns (check-back).** Healthcare analogy, search summary only ([AHRQ](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/checkback.html), snippet). Doubles talk time, and catches mishearing, not wrong beliefs.
- **Fixed Arabic template lines, pre-rendered clips, pronunciation dictionary (M).** Needs a native reviewer. No source says how aliases behave in Arabic.
- **Printed reset stickers, QR codes, hand-written "could match" lists.** Spot's marker idea is sound, but this is physical work, permission, and more mapping. Best fix for look-alike places after the hackathon.
- **Speech-detector gate in the bridge, and echo removal with a reference channel (M, L).** The numbers are guesses and the robot's mic layout is unknown. Record row 7 clips first. Offline test only.
- **Status screen with live captions, colour light.** Recommended by the mall authors ([mall study](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04929v1), read), but outside what we can change tonight. Good for the pitch.
- **Phone handover (`transfer_to_number`) and new stacks (Pipecat, LiveKit, Smart Turn).** New services. We borrow patterns only.
- **"Where am I" tool or stop log.** The route tool is stateless and the chat holds the stops. Only a trained model was shown to gain from longer history ([CVDN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04957), read).

## Conclusion

The sources say our design is sound and our gap is measurement. Every wording rule here rests on typed outdoor text or small studies. Nobody has tested spoken one-step directions to a walking operator indoors. Tonight's tests, plus one stopwatch run on a real leg, will be better evidence than anything in these notes.

Two lessons cut across the sources. In the real mall study, overlapping speech was the biggest cause of conflict (81 cases, ahead of system errors at 52 and wrong route guidance at 18), and a stated reason repaired trust in a small study (7.89 against 6.21 on a 10-point scale, 38 people) ([mall study](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04929v1), [Webb et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21716v1), read). So timing and saying why look cheaper than smarter routing. And because the live model is small, write each prompt rule together with the test that checks it.
